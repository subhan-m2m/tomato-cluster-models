1 · The decision this experiment supports

Trusses or visual clusters?
Choose what helps pruning.

We want to compare two ways of describing tomato groups: which can the model identify reliably, and which helps a grower review fruit/flower thinning within a truss?

Visual cluster: nearby tomatoes grouped by appearance. Truss: a fruit or flower group with a confirmed shared fruiting stem. These are our proposed experiment definitions; growers may use the names interchangeably.

6 · Choose the useful unit for AgriTwin

Compare recognition and usefulness for thinning.

The winner must identify the correct fruiting unit and help a grower review fruit/flower thinning. Count accuracy is only one part of that choice.

  1. Use photos from the thinning stage

    Include flowers and young fruit. Agree with a grower what information is needed to review thinning within a truss.

  2. Label both on the same photos

    Keep visual groups and verified trusses as separate label versions. Record fruit/flower membership; mark unclear connections as uncertain.

  3. Make a fair comparison

    Train each approach with comparable settings. Test on the same new scenes; check missed groups, wrong merges, splits, and counts.

  4. Review usefulness with a grower

    Does the group contain fruit/flowers from one truss? Can its members be counted and reviewed without mixing neighboring trusses?

Team takeaway: the cluster baseline improved with the full annotations. Next we compare it with verified trusses and choose the representation that preserves the grouping needed for fruit/flower thinning.

An easier box to detect is useful only if it represents the intended truss. Current counts do not identify which fruit/flowers to remove; those decisions also need their condition and a grower’s thinning rules.
